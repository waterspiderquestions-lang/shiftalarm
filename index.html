Questions, answered

The stuff people actually ask before trusting an app with their paycheck.

Will the alarm actually go off?
That's the whole reason ShiftAlarm exists. It books exact alarms with Android's own clock, adds backup alarms after your main one, gives an "out the door" warning, and runs a built-in reliability check that flags any phone setting that could silence your alarm — before it costs you a shift.
Does it work for rotating shifts and nights?
Yes — that's what it's built for. Paint any rotation (4-on-4-off, DuPont, or your own pattern) in two taps, or drag shift chips onto days and copy/paste them around. Your alarms follow your roster, not a Monday-to-Friday repeat.
Is it free? Any ads or subscription?
Free, and no ads — ever. No account either. An optional one-time Pro upgrade is planned for later (never a subscription), and everyone in early access keeps everything they already have.
Does it drain my battery?
No. The whole app is under 15 MB, alarms are handed to the system clock, and there's nothing running in the background watching you. It's built to sit quietly until it's needed.
Does it work offline?
Yes. Your schedule, alarms, and pay data live on your phone and work with no connection at all. The only feature that briefly needs internet is the snow early-wake forecast check.
What is snow early wake?
Before your alarm, ShiftAlarm checks the forecast. If heavy snow is coming, it wakes you earlier by the custom buffer you choose — with a reminder to shovel. It only ever wakes you earlier, never later.
Can it track my pay and overtime?
Yes. Set your own regular hours (not everyone is 40 a week), unpaid breaks, and per-shift rates — then export a clean PDF report for payroll. Know your paycheck before payday.
Is my data private?
Everything stays on your device. No account, no cloud sync of your roster, nothing sold. Read the full privacy policy — it's short, because there's nothing to hide.
Is there an iPhone version?
Not yet — ShiftAlarm is Android-first, because that's where alarm reliability needed fixing most. iPhone is on the roadmap after launch.
How do I become a tester?
Fill in the 30-second early access form and you'll get your invite as soon as a testing slot opens.

Help & quick fixes

My phone won't let me allow "Display over other apps"
This happens on newer Android versions when an app is installed directly (sideloaded) instead of from Google Play. Fix: go to Settings → Apps → ShiftAlarm, tap the ⋮ menu (top right), choose "Allow restricted settings", then enable Display over other apps. Once ShiftAlarm is installed from Google Play, this block doesn't apply at all.
My alarm didn't ring — what do I check?
Open ShiftAlarm and run the built-in reliability check — it flags exactly which phone setting is in the way. The most common culprit is battery saving: go to Settings → Apps → ShiftAlarm → Battery and set it to Unrestricted so the system can't delay your alarm.
How do I set up my rotation?
On the calendar, pick your pattern (4-on-4-off, DuPont, or build a custom one) and paint it in two taps. Or just drag shift chips onto the days you work — no rotation needed.
How do I change or remove a shift?
Tap any day to edit it. To clear a day, drag the erase chip onto it (or drag the day to the erase zone). Deleting a day also clears its repeating alarms.
The alarm starts too loud (or too quiet)
In alarm settings, choose your wake style. Gentle mode fades in slowly — the volume ramps up over a full 5 minutes, not 30 seconds. Heavy sleepers can pick max volume with math missions instead.
How do I move everything to a new phone?
Use Backup & Restore: export a backup file on your old phone, copy it to the new one, and import. Your whole schedule, shift templates, and settings come across.
How do I print or share my schedule?
Open the menu and choose Print schedule to get a clean PDF you can print, text to family, or hand to your boss. You can also sync to Google Calendar or export a calendar file.
Something's broken — how do I report it?
Email waterspider.questions@gmail.com with what happened and your phone model. Bugs from early testers get fixed first — that's the deal.
